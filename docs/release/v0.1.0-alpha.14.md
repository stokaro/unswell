## The published binary and the measured revisions

The check of a revision was measured with a build of the branch of its
change, before the release. The binary from the published macOS ARM64 archive
was run on the same texts again, against the
[record](../../research/reviews/2026-11-15-revision-check/RESULTS.md):

| What | Pages | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `unswell check`, against the report that a reviser was given | 86 for each of the two revisers | The same report on every page |
| Index of each revised page and of its revision | 86 for each of the two revisers | Every one the same |
| `unswell compare`, from the kept answers of the judge | 30 pairs | The same verdict, sound, statements, losses, and em dashes on every pair |

The third row needs a word. One judge of the check was a local program, and
each of its answers was kept under its prompt. A binary that sends the same
prompts gets the same answers back without a call to the model, and it has to
reach the same verdict from them. That worked for the 30 pairs whose pages are
texts that a model wrote under a style guide. For the 27 posts of the
style-guide set the kept answers were replaced by the later run on damaged
copies, which asked about the same pages again, so those pairs could not be
replayed. The pairs of the other judge were answered by an endpoint, and its
answers were not kept.

The measured build reported a code span as lost on one post, where the
revision had kept it; the record says so. The release has the correction. That
post is among the 27 that could not be replayed.

The checks are `scripts/reproduce.py` of this record and of the
[earlier one](../../research/reviews/2026-11-15-style-guide-text/README.md).
They need the texts, which stay with the maintainer.
