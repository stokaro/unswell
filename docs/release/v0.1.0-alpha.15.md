## The published binary and the measured copies

The comparison of sentences was measured with a build of the branch of its
change, before the release. The binary from the published macOS ARM64 archive
was run on the same texts again, against the
[record](../../research/reviews/2026-11-15-sentence-check/RESULTS.md):

| What | Pages | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| The comparison of sentences without a model, on every copy of the record | 421 | The same suspects on every copy |
| `unswell compare`, from the kept answers of the judge | 30 pairs | The same verdict, sound, statements, sentences, losses, and em dashes on every pair |
| `unswell check`, against the report that a reviser of the fresh texts was given | 30 for each of the two revisers | The same report and the same index on every page |

The first row needs no model, so it covers every copy: the damaged and the
harmless ones, of the fitting half and of the measured part.

The second row is a replay. One judge is a local program, and each of its
answers is kept under its prompt; a binary that sends the same prompts gets
the same answers back without a call to the model. That works for the 30
revisions whose pages had no damaged copy. For a page with damaged copies the
later runs asked about the same page again and replaced the kept answer, so
those pairs cannot be replayed: on 27 such revisions of the posts the binary
was given a new list of statements for 25 and reached another verdict on 3.
The answers of the other judge come from an endpoint and were not kept.

The checks are `scripts/audit.py` and `scripts/reproduce.py` of the record,
and `scripts/reproduce.py` of the
[record of the second reading](../../research/reviews/2026-11-15-style-guide-text/README.md).
They need the texts, which stay with the maintainer.
