## The published binary and the measured copies

The rule that names the words a revision leaves out of a sentence was
measured with a build of the branch of its change, before the release. The
binary from the published macOS ARM64 archive was run on the same texts
again, against the
[record](../../research/reviews/2026-11-16-left-out-words/RESULTS.md):

| What | Pages | Result |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| The comparison of sentences without a model, on every copy of the record | 760 | The same suspects, the same passages, and the same left-out words on every copy |
| `unswell compare`, from the kept answers of the judge | 57 revisions | The same verdict, sound, statements, sentences, losses, and em dashes on every pair |

The first row needs no model, so it covers every copy: the damaged, the
harmless, and the trimmed ones, of the fitting half and of the measured part.
It also shows the two things that the command now does on its own. On each of
the 100 copies with a sentence removed, the published binary reports the
sentence without a question to a model. On each of the 100 trimmed copies it
names the padding as the words that were left out.

The second row is a replay. One judge is a local program, and each of its
answers is kept under its prompt; a binary that sends the same prompts gets
the same answers back without a call to the model. That covers the revisions
that this judge checked: 27 of posts and 30 of fresh texts. The damaged and
the trimmed copies cannot be replayed: several copies of one page ask the
same question about that page, and a repeated question gets a new answer. The
answers of the other judge come from an endpoint and were not kept.

The published binary differs from the measured build in the help text of the
command.

The checks are `scripts/audit.py` and `scripts/reproduce.py` of the record.
They need the texts, which stay with the maintainer.
