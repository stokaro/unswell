# GEN edit-operation transfer: preregistration

Date: November 8, 2026. This is a model-free origin-feature feasibility study, separate from issue #349's editorial diagnosis gates. It is motivated by [Shan, Lee, and Hao, *AI Writers Have a Consistent Stylometric Footprint, but AI Editors Do Not*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27855v2), which reports different feature directions for full generation and editing. Its published accuracy is not an Unswell result. No origin label is inferred for an individual document, and no runtime score or rule changes in this study.

A group is `(source, prompt_id)`. There are five declared source domains. `seed_id` is global, with 200-ID blocks per domain in the declared order; it is not a per-domain zero-based index. The edited row's `seed_text` must match the corresponding human text after whitespace normalization; record any mismatch and exclude that row from paired analysis. Do not infer unique authorship or fact-preserving revision from the corpus label. Duplicate `(group, model)` generated variants stay visible; select the lexicographically smallest UTF-8 text SHA-256 for one source/model pair, without looking at feature values. Missing variants abstain.

The edited acquisition keeps every row with `target_ratio == 30`, regardless of measured edit ratio, model, prompt, or category. This is a fixed sampling decision, not a claim that 30% of words actually changed. Do not silently filter unfavorable edit types. Retain all distinct edit attempts in the selected stream and report duplicate keys and any source mismatch. The full edited file is consumed once into a SHA-256-verified, local compressed selection; its 50% and 70% target rows are not scored.

## Frozen feature and split plan

Reuse the unchanged Unswell `richness` and `entropy` definitions from `research/origin/runs/2026-11-07-richness-screen/run.py`. Add a fixed English closed-class-word fraction on the same extracted prose tokens: count occurrences of a prespecified function-word lexicon and divide by prose tokens. This is a paper-inspired proxy for lower lexical density, not the paper's exact NLTK tokenizer or feature implementation. Pin that lexicon and the scoring script before scoring.

For each domain, sort its 1,000 human `prompt_id` values by SHA-256 of `unswell-gen-edit-v1|source|prompt_id`, breaking ties by numeric ID. Assign the first 500 to calibration and the last 500 to test. All generated and edited variants inherit the human source's assignment. No source group crosses the boundary. Set feature directions before scoring: higher richness and entropy for full generation; higher closed-class fraction and lower entropy for editing. Use human-only calibration thresholds. A 99th-percentile threshold is the highest score exceeded by at most 1% of eligible calibration humans. Compare each machine variant with its source-matched human, and report test-human false-positive rate independently.

The primary view is the first 100 prose tokens of each human/machine side, requiring both sides to have at least 100 tokens. Full prose is secondary and may reflect length and document structure. Report pair eligibility and abstention, source groups, pooled AUROC, paired wins with ties as half, test-human false-positive rate, and true-positive rate at the fixed threshold. Break out all five domains, all available generators/editors, and all editing categories; do not pool correlated variants as independent sources. Also report measured edit-ratio distributions and all missing or duplicate source/model identities.

## Decision boundary

For each of the two preregistered editing features (closed-class fraction and reverse entropy) to pass this *feasibility* screen, require at least 80% eligible test pairs, at least 0.65 pooled AUROC, at least 10% TPR at the human-calibrated threshold, at most 2% observed test-human FPR, and a nonreversed paired direction in at least four of five domains. AUROC over variants is descriptive; variants sharing a human source are correlated, so no row-level confidence interval or independent-sample claim is valid. Richness for editing and full-prose views are secondary descriptions, not replacement primary hypotheses. The full-generation channel is a separate comparison. Passing GEN would only justify a separately frozen, source- and prompt-disjoint external transfer test, especially on technical prose. Failing means the feature is retired without changing a detector. No result from this origin study contributes to #349 FULL-event recall, accepted-findings fraction, coverage, or revision safety.
